Projects

QuickDCE: native-format low-light enhancement for automotive cameras

Research project with Scania, advised by Prof. Yu LinJun 2026 – present

Night-time images from vehicle camera monitor systems are dark and noisy, and the enhancement has to run in real time on the vehicle. I built a lightweight enhancement pipeline for this setting:

  • Works directly on the camera's native NV12 format and enhances luminance only, keeping the cost low.
  • Base/detail decomposition to suppress noise in dark flat regions.
  • Reference-free evaluation protocol; the model is quantized and deployed with the Qualcomm QNN SDK.

Lifelong open-set person re-identification on an autonomous patrol robot

Advanced Institute of Information Technology, Peking University · Team lead: Dr. Zhao WangFeb – Jun 2026

I worked on a proactive companion robot system built on a Unitree Go2 quadruped. I led the open-set identity module, which lets the robot recognize people it has met before, notice people it has never seen, and keep learning new identities over time. I also took part in other parts of the system.

Building an LLM training system from scratch

Self-directed project based on Stanford CS336Sep 2025 – Jan 2026

Following Stanford's CS336 course, I implemented the main parts of a language model training system myself, from the tokenizer and Transformer model to the training loop, to understand how large models are built and trained in practice.

Beyond research

I believe a good life is more than work. Outside the lab, I spend my time reading, teaching, and staying active.

Reading

I enjoy classic literature and have read nearly a hundred classic novels, most of them in Chinese translation. I keep coming back to a few kinds of stories: 19th-century novels about people struggling against the society around them, Russian novels that ask hard questions about conscience and freedom, existentialist works, and dystopian novels such as 1984.

I am also interested in philosophy. I have read a history of Western philosophy, from Socrates and Plato through Descartes, Locke, and Hume to Kant and Hegel. I am most drawn to Kant’s idea that every person should be treated as an end, never merely as a means, which I first explored in depth through Michael Sandel’s Harvard course Justice. I also enjoy existentialist thinkers such as Kierkegaard, Nietzsche, and Sartre.

Tutoring and giving back

I try to be there for people when life gets hard. Over the years, I have supported at least 6 classmates and students through some of the hardest periods of their lives, including struggles with depression. Mostly this meant being present: long chats, late-night phone calls, and listening more than talking.

In my spare time, I also tutor children in their schoolwork. Explaining an idea to someone else is often how I find out whether I really understand it.

I also donate blood. It is a small thing, but it is one of the simplest ways I know to help a stranger.
