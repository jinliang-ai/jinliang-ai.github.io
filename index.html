Research Interests

I am interested in making visual perception reliable outside the lab. My current interests include:

  • Computer vision under degraded imaging — low-light enhancement, noise, and evaluation without ground truth.
  • Efficient on-device perception — quantization and deployment on embedded and automotive hardware.
  • Perception for robots — recognizing and tracking people over time in open, changing environments.

Projects

Native-Format Low-Light Enhancement for Automotive Cameras

Industry collaboration with Scania · ZJUI · Jun 2026 – present

Night-time images from vehicle camera monitor systems are dark and noisy, and the enhancement has to run in real time on the vehicle. I built a lightweight pipeline that works directly in the camera's native NV12 format and enhances brightness only, which keeps the cost low. I added a base/detail decomposition to reduce noise in dark flat regions, and designed an evaluation protocol that does not need clean reference images. The model is quantized and deployed with the Qualcomm QNN SDK.

Lifelong Open-Set Person Re-Identification on a Quadruped Robot

Research Intern · Advanced Institute of Information Technology, Peking University · Feb – Jun 2026

I worked on a proactive companion robot system built on a Unitree Go2 quadruped. I led the open-set identity module, which lets the robot recognize people it has met before, notice people it has never seen, and keep learning new identities over time. I also took part in other parts of the system.

Building an LLM Training System from Scratch

Self-directed project based on Stanford CS336 · Sep 2025 – Jan 2026

Following Stanford's CS336 course, I implemented the main parts of a language model training system myself, from the tokenizer and Transformer model to the training loop, to understand how large models are built and trained in practice.
